## Orientation

Photo compass headings: approximately 291°, 319° and 311° true, looking west-northwest to northwest. The GPS positions lie around 15–20 metres east of the project’s household 22 centre, with reported horizontal accuracy around 4–5 metres. These support an eastern roadside approach and a broadly southeast-facing photographed brick frontage. Compass and GPS are not survey measurements.

The short video shows the brick section, adjoining white section, garden and trees. Earth shows the long modern house at the western edge of the surviving built group. The main Black Brook woodland is west of this house, with woodland also extending south around the wider site. Modern canopy is evidence of current conditions, not a map of 1865 trees.

## Important oral-history clue

Around 02:09–02:19 in IMG_2411, the speaker says the bathroom was added at the end where an old chainshop had stood. They say a tree occupied the spot when they arrived, and date the extension to 1973. This makes the white, flat-roofed addition visible in IMG_2412 a plausible location reference for the former workshop. It appears to be at the eastern/roadside end. This identification is an inference; exact workshop dimensions and orientation are not established.

The transcription also records the white section as 1896 and the red-fronted section as 1926. Treat these as the speaker’s reported dates, not independently confirmed construction dates. They are a reason not to copy the modern house wholesale into 1865.

The speaker describes a nearby complex as built in 1977, on previously rough land. This helps explain why the modern built edge and historic household arrangement differ. It does not establish the identity or demolition date of project households 13 or 26.

## Model implications

- Keep historical households 13 and 26 in the 1865 scene. Present-day absence or canopy concealment does not justify removal.
- Household 13 is about 20 m west of 22; household 26 is roughly 14 m west and 16 m north in the project data.
- A small domestic chainshop belongs with Henry’s home as a provisional oral-history detail, separate from the surviving communal forge landmark.
- Prefer the eastern/roadside end for the small shop if modelling now. Mark its footprint and date as interpreted, rather than claiming a surveyed 1865 workshop.
- Replace evenly scattered trees with an irregular woodland belt along the western brook valley and southern margin, leaving historic dwelling plots and work yards open.
- Preserve the current historical map coordinates while making the garden and tree boundary feel closer to the house.

Implemented after Tom’s approval: a roughly 5 × 3.6 m shop at Henry’s eastern/roadside gable, using the main forge’s materials and geometry with one chimney, hearth glow and smoke. The footprint is interpreted. Added grouped woodland along the brook and southern margin, with clear space around historical houses and roads. Historical households 13 and 26 remain.
