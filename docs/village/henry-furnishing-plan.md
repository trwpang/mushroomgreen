# Henry’s home — furnishing by use

## Evidence and intent

The record for Henry Weaver (No. 22) states **two occupants in the 1861 census**. It does not list names, ages, furniture, or the 1865 occupants. The scene retains its current interpreted footprint (10.56 × 6.44 m). These furnishings are an interpretation, not an inventory of Henry’s possessions.

The previous pass counted objects but did not give the room a practical domestic arrangement. In particular, sewing occupied the dining table, a bench stood away from the wall, kitchen storage was too small, and the repeated floor kit looked like a roof surface.

## New reference review

- [Oak Cottage kitchen, reconstructed c.1840, David Dixon](https://www.geograph.org.uk/photo/3001493): viewed directly. A scrubbed preparation table stands against the wall. Containers occupy shelves above. An apron and basket hang near the door. The large flat flags provide a useful comparison with the smaller quarry tiles.
- [Pitt’s Cottage living kitchen, BCLM](https://www.sloweurope.com/community/media/living-room-kitchen-in-pitts-cottage-black-country-museum.3536/): viewed directly. The range has close shelves, mantel plates, fire tools, and stored books. [BCLM dates the cottage to around the 1890s](https://bclm.com/plan-your-visit/trails/start-in-1712/), so this informs spatial logic rather than exact 1865 decoration.
- [BCLM kitchen photograph, captioned 1910](https://www.pinterest.com/pin/384494886930191300/): viewed the full photograph. Tiles are flat squares with narrow joints. Red and dark tiles form a level floor beneath the table. The model uses simpler red quarry tiles; it does not copy the room’s later ornaments or pattern.
- [Museum of the Home: kitchen Windsor armchair, 1828–1851](https://collections.museumofthehome.org.uk/object4046): period evidence for a wooden kitchen armchair, with shaped arms, turned supports, stretchers, and a solid seat.
- [Soane Museum: early nineteenth-century Windsor armchair](https://collections.soane.org/object-xf278): period evidence for hoop-backed kitchen chairs. The new meshes are original interpretations, not scans or exact reproductions.

## Floor correction

The room uses one continuous 9-inch (228.6 mm) square grid with narrow joints. Edge tiles are cut to the room. All walking faces share the same height. Shallow rounded shoulders and small corner losses soften the regular grid without roof-like overlaps.

The September 20 wear pass replaces the new-looking uniform finish with muted kiln-colour differences, clay mottling and fine surface relief. Routes between the entrance and household work areas are smoother from repeated use. This is clean wear, not added dirt or stains. Timber and stone floors use separate floor-only materials, so their wear does not spread to furniture or ceilings. Procedural grain fades below pixel size to limit shimmer during movement.

## Wider village pass

The same planner now moves benches and washstands toward walls where routes permit. Laundry baskets move beside washing areas. Larger cottages gain separate preparation tables and food storage. Small cottages retain a shared cooking/eating table. More dining seats are added according to the record and available space. Armchairs enter only where they fit. Sewing machines use separate tables. Cooking shelves use crockery and food containers rather than random sewing tools.

Twelve additional models bring the catalogue to **112**: two armchairs, preparation table, sewing table, water crock and stand, kindling basket, pan rack, sampler, silhouette, mantel clock, vegetable basket, and plate drainer. Existing objects are reused in related groups. Quantity is not a substitute for a useful arrangement.
