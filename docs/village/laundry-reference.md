# Henry’s washing line

Replaced four rectangular boards with a long-sleeved shirt, a waist apron, a plain petticoat and two stockings. These are interpreted household garments, not documented possessions of Henry Weaver.

The shirt uses a short front opening rather than a modern full-length button front. The petticoat is plain, without fashionable lace. Muted cloth colours, woven texture, hems and a pocket keep the garments suitable for a modest household.

References:
- V&A, [Sew your own: customisable shirt](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/sew-your-own-customisable-shirt): the historic role of shirts as washable undergarments and development of front openings.
- National Archives, [Victorian fashion](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/victorians/victorian-fashion/): nineteenth-century cotton and linen shirts.
- National Gallery of Victoria, [Outfit, c. 1860](https://www.ngv.vic.gov.au/explore/collection/work/45459/): period cotton underclothing reference; decorative details were not copied into this working household.

Cloth geometry has folds and uneven hems. A sagging rope and split wooden pegs support the garments. Gentle movement uses the shared scene clock, so Pause, reduced motion and fixed comparison time also control the laundry.
